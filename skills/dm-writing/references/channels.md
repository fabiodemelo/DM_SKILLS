# Channels

Short specs. Per-channel detail goes in the project file when it exists.

**Website pages.** One promise in the H1. Problem, then outcome, then proof, then how to start. One CTA per page, the same CTA across the site unless the project file says otherwise. Meta description under 160 characters, written like a sentence a person would say.

**Articles.** 900 to 1,400 words. Open on an observation, not a topic. Surface the thing that doesn't fit within 200 words. Two to four headings, written as sentences. Name why this is hard to do well without calling us, then stop before the method. Close on a quiet invitation, never a button-style ask in the body.

**LinkedIn.** 100 to 280 words. One or two sentences per paragraph. No emojis, no "thoughts?", no hashtag pile. Link in the first comment. Positioning line in roughly one post in five.

**Replies and comments.** 15 to 80 words. Add something, don't agree. No links to our own content, no positioning line, no tagline. Always a person.

**Email.** Subject says what's inside. First line is the reason for the email. One ask. No "I hope this finds you well," no "just checking in."

**App and UI strings.** Plain verbs, sentence case, no jargon, no internal names. Errors say what happened and what to do next. Empty states say what will appear and how to make it appear. The same outcome-only rule as the website applies to anything visible before login.

**Case studies.** Real client with written permission. Describe the problem and the outcome. Never publish the method. No loss framing that blames the reader ("you are losing").

**Pitch decks and proposals.** Pricing and scope match the locked register. Confidential method detail only under NDA, marked as such.
